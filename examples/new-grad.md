# DECISION.md — Sam's Decision Framework
## New Grad, Software Engineer (First Job)

## Decision Identity
Eager learner navigating the transition from academic to professional decision-making. Tends to over-research and under-act — a habit from school where more preparation always helped. Starting to learn that in the real world, speed and iteration often beat perfect preparation. Open to risk in career decisions (nothing to lose yet) but cautious with money (student loans are real).

## Risk Profile
- Overall: Moderate (learning to be more aggressive)
- Career: Aggressive — this is the time to take big swings and learn fast
- Financial: Conservative — student debt means every dollar matters
- Health: Moderate — knows it matters but doesn't prioritize it enough yet
- Social: Moderate-aggressive — willing to put myself out there, still building confidence
- Technical: Aggressive — happy to learn new stacks, break things, take on stretch projects

## Autonomy Rules
- Act without asking if: part of my assigned task, cost < $20, won't affect team deliverables
- Always ask if: architectural decisions, anything customer-facing, spending money, changes that affect others' work
- Escalation triggers: I'm stuck for more than 2 hours, scope seems bigger than expected, I'm unsure about company norms

## Decision Speed
- Default mode: Deliberate (working on being faster)
- Small decisions: Force myself to decide in 5 minutes. "What's the worst that could happen?"
- Big decisions: 1 week maximum. Talk to 2-3 people I trust, then decide.
- Analysis paralysis tripwire: If I've read more than 5 articles about a decision, I'm procrastinating — just pick one and learn

## Tradeoff Hierarchy
When values conflict, prioritize in this order:
1. Learning and skill development
2. Building relationships and reputation
3. Financial stability (pay off debt)
4. Work-life balance
5. Short-term comfort

## Kill Criteria Defaults
- Review cadence: Monthly career check-in with myself, weekly learning reflection
- Walk-away test: "Am I learning something new every month? Do I respect the people I work with?"
- Sunk cost override: Learning to activate this — remind me that "I've already invested so much" is not a reason to continue
- Job kill criteria: If I haven't learned anything new in 3 months, or if Sunday nights consistently fill me with dread

## Calibration Profile
- Overconfident about: How quickly I can learn a new technology, my understanding of business context
- Underconfident about: My technical skills (I know more than I think), my right to have opinions in meetings
- Debiasing: Ask "what would I advise a friend in this situation?" — I give better advice than I take

## Anti-Patterns
Things I do wrong — catch me when you see these:
- "I don't speak up in meetings because I think I'm too junior" → If I have a thought, share it. Being wrong is fine.
- "I over-research instead of trying" → Set a timer. 30 minutes of research, then start building.
- "I compare myself to senior engineers and feel behind" → Compare to where I was 3 months ago, not to others.
- "I say yes to everything to seem helpful" → Check my plate first. Saying no to one thing means saying yes to something better.
- "I optimize for not looking dumb" → Looking dumb while learning is better than looking smart while stagnating.

## Domain Rules
### Work
- Ask questions early and often. The cost of asking is always lower than the cost of building the wrong thing.
- Default to over-communicating, not under-communicating
- Ship small PRs frequently. Don't hide in a branch for 2 weeks.
- Say "I don't know, but I'll find out" — it's always respected

### Personal
- Protect at least one evening a week for non-work activities
- Maintain friendships outside of work — they keep perspective
- Invest in one hobby that has nothing to do with tech

### Financial
- Pay minimums on all debt, throw extras at highest interest rate
- Build emergency fund to $5k before investing
- Don't lifestyle-inflate with the first real paycheck

## Meta-Rules
- When in doubt: Ask someone with more experience, then make my own call
- Confidence threshold for autonomous action: 70% (learning to trust myself)
- Update frequency: Monthly for the first year, then quarterly
