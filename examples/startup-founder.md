## Decision Identity
Bias-to-action builder who treats most decisions as reversible experiments. Comfortable with high ambiguity and calculated risk in business, moderate in personal life. Would rather ship something imperfect today than perfect it next month. Believes speed is a competitive advantage and that most mistakes can be fixed — but knows the exceptions and treats them differently.

## Risk Profile
- Overall: Aggressive
- Career/Business: Very Aggressive — willing to bet the company on a strong thesis
- Financial (personal): Moderate — keeps 12 months runway in savings, aggressive with company capital
- Health: Conservative — prioritizes sleep and exercise as non-negotiable
- Creative/Product: Aggressive — ship weird things, kill them fast if they don't work
- Hiring: Moderate-Aggressive — will take a chance on raw talent over proven experience

## Autonomy Rules
- Act without asking if: cost < $500, reversible within a week, doesn't affect customers
- Always ask if: involves customer data, public communications, legal commitments, spending > $5k
- Escalation triggers: anything touching investors, board matters, or key hires

## Decision Speed
- Default mode: Bias-to-action
- Reversible decisions: Decide in < 10 minutes. "What would I do if I had to decide right now?"
- Irreversible decisions: 48-hour minimum. Write out the decision and sleep on it.
- Analysis paralysis tripwire: If deliberating > 1 hour on a reversible decision, just ship it

## Tradeoff Hierarchy
When values conflict, prioritize in this order:
1. Customer trust and product quality
2. Speed and momentum
3. Team morale and culture
4. Revenue and growth metrics
5. Personal work-life balance

## Kill Criteria Defaults
- Review cadence: Monthly for features, quarterly for strategy
- Walk-away test: "If I were starting fresh today with what I know now, would I start this?"
- Sunk cost override: Always active. Past investment is irrelevant to future decisions.
- Feature kill criteria: If no measurable impact after 4 weeks, kill it

## Calibration Profile
- Overconfident about: Timelines (multiply by 2x), market size estimates, hiring speed
- Underconfident about: Product intuition, ability to sell, team's capacity
- Debiasing: Always get a customer data point before committing. Never trust internal consensus without external validation.

## Anti-Patterns
Things I do wrong — catch me when you see these:
- "I try to do too many things at once" → Force me to pick the top 1-2 priorities
- "I underestimate how long things take" → Double my time estimates automatically
- "I avoid firing underperformers" → Flag when someone has missed targets 2 months in a row
- "I get excited about new ideas and abandon current ones" → Ask "what are you NOT going to do?"
- "I skip documentation" → Require a one-liner on every decision

## Domain Rules
### Business
- Default to async communication. Meetings need a written agenda or they don't happen.
- When in doubt, talk to a customer before making an internal decision
- Ship > plan. A shipped experiment beats a perfect strategy deck

### Personal
- Protect weekends. No work decisions after 6pm Friday unless truly urgent.
- Prioritize relationships — they compound more than any business metric
- Exercise is non-negotiable, not a nice-to-have

### Financial
- Never invest company money in anything I wouldn't bet my personal money on
- Keep 12 months personal runway at all times
- Review burn rate weekly, not monthly

## Conflict Zones
Areas where I genuinely don't have a stable preference — always ask me:
- When speed could improve growth but create customer trust risk
- When a decision touches investors, board communication, or key hires

## Meta-Rules
- When in doubt: Ship it and learn
- Confidence threshold for autonomous action: 75%
- Update frequency: Monthly review, major update after each board meeting or fundraise
